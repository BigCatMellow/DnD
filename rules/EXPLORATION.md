# Exploration and social procedures

Exploration borrows Pathfinder's emphasis on clear player activity **without importing its three-action combat rules**.

## Exploration approach
When traveling or investigating, choose a default focus: **Scout** (notice threats), **Search** (thorough examination, slower), **Sneak** (avoid being seen), **Track** (follow traces), **Survey** (map/navigation), **Converse** (learn rumors and intentions), or **Guard** (watch while others work). This is descriptive; anyone can change focus anytime. One lead roll is enough when the whole group is doing the same thing.

## Time and risk
Track meaningful time in 10-minute **turns** in confined locations, and travel legs outside. Announce time costs. Searching an area thoroughly normally costs one turn; careful scouting also costs one turn. Running/forced travel is faster but may increase detection. When a consequence is warranted, advance the local **pressure clock** in the scene data. Never roll for random encounters merely because ten minutes passed.

## Information design
- **Essential clue:** always available if the character reasonably examines the relevant place/person. A check may supply extra context or reduce cost, but cannot erase the only path forward.
- **Optional clue:** may be earned through a check, bargain, preparation or alternate route.
- **Three paths:** important problems should permit at least two substantially distinct solutions (talk, stealth, force, environment), ideally three.
- **Fail-forward:** narrate the attempted progress and attach visible cost (noise, lost time, limited trust, damaged gear, attention, fatigue).

## Social interactions
NPCs have a goal, fear and leverage in [setting/PEOPLE.md](../setting/PEOPLE.md). The player must say what they offer, ask or threaten. No single Persuasion roll mind-controls an NPC; checks resolve plausible uncertainty. Success may shift a stated attitude by one step: hostile -> wary -> open -> ally. Strong success may additionally secure a concession; malicious lies can damage trust when exposed.

## Travel and supplies
The initial adventure uses local journeys of 1–3 hours. One person consumes one ration/day and one water unit/day on extended travel; local trips need no forced accounting unless supplies are fictionally relevant. Darkness without a light source gives disadvantage on sight-reliant Perception checks and may make certain tasks impossible. Short distances do not need separate exhaustion rules. Long-distance hazards introduced later need a versioned rules amendment first.

## Clocks
A clock has a name, current segments and maximum. It advances **only for fictional causes** described before or learned through play. At maximum, the explicit event occurs. A clock is not automatic punishment for questioning the GM. Starting scenario clock: `bell_attention`, 0 of 4.
